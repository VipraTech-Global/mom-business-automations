Audit, verification, closure with a photo, auditor check, final close

Every NC and PC is followed through five steps, so a finding is closed only once it has been fixed and checked.

  1. Audit: the auditor submits the audit.
  2. Verification: the department head reviews it, corrects anything needed, and submits it.
  3. Closure: the auditee fixes each point and closes it with a remark and a photo.
  4. Auditor check: the auditor checks each fix on the floor against its photo.
  5. Final close: the department head verifies and closes the audit.

Each point shows as Not closed, In progress or Closed all the way through.

Return verification, from the partner's request to the credit note.

PointWhat it does
Logins for channel partners, logistics, factory and finance

Everyone involved in a return works in the app with their own login, so each step is recorded by the person who did it.

  • Channel partner: raises the return request.
  • MOM's logistics person: enters the pickup and transport details.
  • Factory: receives and inspects the goods, and records the facts.
  • Finance: raises the credit note.
The partner gives the batch number and reason; the details fill in from an Excel upload

Every return starts as a request from the channel partner and becomes one tracked record, all the way to the credit note.

  • The partner enters the batch number and the reason for the return.
  • Product, quantity, bill and address details fill in from MOM's order data. In Base and Standard this comes from an Excel upload of the ERP export. In Premium it comes straight from the ERP.
  • One request can cover several products from the same lot.
Pickup with dates and the LR copy, then receipt at the factory with photos and video
  1. Logistics enters the pickup date, the delivery date and the LR copy.
  2. The factory checks the goods against the LR copy, inspects them and accepts them.
  3. It records any shortage, damage or other company's product, with photos, video, remarks and batch numbers.

When the factory accepts the goods, its remarks are emailed to the people concerned.

The findings are confirmed, then finance raises the credit note
  1. Logistics and the channel partner each confirm the factory's findings.
  2. Finance gets an email with a link, raises the credit note and enters its number.
  3. The return closes, with its photos and remarks attached.

Every credit note is backed by what the factory actually received.

A return stuck at one step too long goes to its owner, then to their manager

A return that sits at one step for longer than a set number of days is raised by itself.

  1. The person who owns that step is reminded.
  2. If it still does not move, their manager is told.

MOM sets the number of days. No return waits at a step without anyone noticing.

Goods that reach the factory without a request are handled too

Sometimes goods reach the factory before anyone has raised a request.

  • The factory raises the request itself and fills in the channel partner and logistics details.
  • It then receives the goods as usual, and the approvals and credit note follow.

No return goes unrecorded, however it arrives.

A returns report carrying the ERP references
  • For each return and in total: pieces received, accepted and rechecked, the reason, and whether a credit note was raised.
  • Format: downloads in MOM's format, with ERP references that show exactly which products came back.

To discuss

PointWhat we need to know
Which inspections the plant has; the app can build one ready file for each visit

When an FSSAI officer, a certification auditor or a customer's quality team visits, they ask for written proof:

  • calibration certificates for the past year,
  • recent audit reports, with proof that each NC was closed,
  • the shelf-life study behind a product's claimed life,
  • copies of licences, with their validity.

Today this is gathered by hand from folders, emails and spreadsheets. In the app, all of it is already stored.

The inspection pack gathers it in one step. Pick a period and the modules to include, and the app builds a single PDF with a cover page, an index, and every certificate, audit report (with its closure photos and sign-offs) and shelf-life result in order, ready to email or print.

What we would like to know

  • Which outside audits or inspections does the plant have, and how often?
  • What do they usually ask to see, and how much preparation does a visit take today?
  • Would one ready-made file help, or do you prefer to show records on screen during the visit?
Whether phones get a signal everywhere, which decides how much offline drafts matter

The app saves records over the phone's mobile data or Wi-Fi. If some areas have no network, people there cannot submit a checklist or a result on the spot. Offline drafts (in Premium) solve this.

What we would like to know

  • Are there areas, such as cold rooms, stores or the production floor, with no mobile signal or Wi-Fi?
  • Is there plant Wi-Fi that staff phones can use?
What access MOM IT can give for the ERP link in Premium

ERP auto-fill and credit-note write-back in Premium need access to MOM's ERP, given by MOM IT. Base and Standard do not need it: order and batch details come in by Excel upload.

What we would like to know

  • Which ERP does MOM use?
  • Who in MOM IT can give access?
  • Can the app read orders and batches, and write credit-note numbers back?
Whether MOM uses Slack, Teams or neither, which decides where chat alerts go

In Standard and Premium, alerts can also go to MOM's team chat app, and in Premium people can act from it.

What we would like to know

  • Does MOM use Slack, Microsoft Teams, or neither?

If neither, alerts go by email, WhatsApp and SMS only.
